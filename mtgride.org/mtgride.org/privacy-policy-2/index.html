Definitions

Personal Data: refers to any information pertaining to an identified or identifiable individual (referred to as the ‘data subject’). An identifiable individual is someone who can be directly or indirectly identified, particularly through an identifier such as a name, identification number, location data, online identifier, or through factors specific to their physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity.

Processing: encompasses any operation or series of operations carried out on personal data or sets of personal data, whether automated or not. These operations include collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination, or any other means of making the data available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure, or destruction.

Restriction of Processing: denotes the act of marking stored personal data with the intention of limiting their processing in the future.

Controller: refers to a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other entity that, either alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of processing personal data. In cases where Union or Member State law determines the purposes and means of such processing, the controller or the specific criteria for their appointment may be established by Union or Member State law.

Processor: signifies a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other entity that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.

Consent of the Data Subject: signifies the voluntary, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of the data subject’s wishes. This consent can be expressed through a statement or a clear affirmative action, indicating agreement to the processing of personal data concerning the data subject.

  1. Cookies

Cookies are small text files containing a combination of numbers and letters that are automatically stored on your hard disk each time you visit a specific webpage. These files serve the purpose of identifying your browser when you revisit the same website. Moreover, cookies have the ability to retain user preferences and other relevant information.

In order to manage your privacy settings, you have the option to configure your browser to either reject all cookies or notify you whenever a cookie is received. However, it is important to note that certain website features or services may not operate optimally if you choose to decline the receipt of cookies. Rest assured, any data collected through this process cannot be used to personally identify individual website users. Furthermore, cookies are incapable of executing programs or transmitting viruses to your computer.

  1. Your Rights

(a) The Right to Access

You have the right to confirm whether or not we process your personal data. If we do, you also have the right to access your personal data, along with additional information. This additional information includes details about the purposes of the processing, the categories of personal data involved, and the recipients of the personal data. As long as it does not infringe upon the rights and freedoms of others, we will provide you with a copy of your personal data.

(b) The Right to Rectification

You have the right to request the rectification of any inaccurate personal data about you. Additionally, considering the purposes of the processing, you can request the completion of any incomplete personal data.

(c) The Right to Erasure

Under certain circumstances, you have the right to request the erasure of your personal data. This applies when it is determined that the personal data is no longer necessary for the purposes it was collected or processed, when you withdraw your permission for authorization-based processing, when you object to the processing under specific rules of applicable data protection law, or when it is established that the personal data has been unlawfully processed. However, there are exceptions to the right to erasure. These exceptions include cases where processing is necessary for the exercise of the right to freedom of expression and information, for compliance with a legal obligation, for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims, or for reasons of public interest.

(d) The Right to Restrict Processing

In certain circumstances, you have the right to restrict the processing of your personal data. This may apply when you contest the accuracy of the personal data, when the processing is unlawful but you oppose its erasure, when we no longer need the personal data for our processing purposes but you require it for legal claims, or when you have objected to the processing and verification of that objection is pending. If processing is restricted based on these grounds, we will continue to store your personal data and only process it for legal claims, protection of rights, or important public interest reasons.

(e) The Right to Object to Processing

You have the right to object to the processing of your personal data if it is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest, the exercise of official authority vested in us, or for legitimate interests pursued by us or a third party. If you object, we will stop processing your personal information unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights, and freedoms, or if the processing is for legal claims.

You also have the right to object to the processing of your personal data for direct marketing purposes, including profiling for such purposes. If you object, we will cease processing your personal data for this purpose.

Furthermore, you have the right to object to the processing of your personal data for scientific, historical research, or statistical purposes, based on grounds relating to your particular situation, unless the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

(f) The Right to Data Portability

You have the right to obtain the personal information provided to us, provided that:

– The processing is based on authorization; or

– The processing is carried out by automated means.

In such cases, you have the right to receive your personal data from us in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format. However, please note that this right does not apply if it would adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others.

(g) The Right to Complain to a Supervisory Authority

If you believe that the processing of your personal information violates data protection laws, you have the legal right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority responsible for data protection. You can do so in the EU member state where you usually reside, your place of work, or the location where the alleged infringement occurred.

(h) The Right to Withdraw Authorization

If the legal basis for processing your personal information is authorization, you have the right to withdraw that authorization at any time. However, please be aware that by doing so, we may not be able to provide you with certain features and functionality of our services. It is important to note that the withdrawal of permission will not affect the lawfulness of processing prior to the withdrawal request.

  1. Notification of Breaches

In the event of unauthorized breaches or intrusions into our systems that may compromise the security of your personal data, we are committed to promptly notifying you. We will take all necessary measures and actions to prevent future occurrences and similar incidents.

  1. Contact Us

If you have any inquiries regarding this Privacy and Data Protection Policy or the processing and usage of personal information in relation to our Software Applications or websites, please do not hesitate to reach out to us. To ensure a thorough and efficient response, kindly include your name and any other relevant information for identification and the complete processing of your request.
